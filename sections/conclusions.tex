Our first observation is that with more constraints added to the adversary, such as random arrivals, competitiveness increases. This is not surprising As these constraints limit the adversary's ability to construct worst case scenario inputs. What is surprising, however, is that in the i.i.d. model the best algorithm is deterministic despite the large advantage that randomized algorithms enjoy in simpler models\\
What can be concluded is that, if the adversary is sufficiently constrained and enough information is given, randomness becomes less important as decisions can be guided\\
The model changes that don't sufficiently constrain the adversary are also surprising, both final degrees, which should convey relatively large amounts of information about the final graph, and the largest delays that also don't trivialize this problem in terms of complexity classes, aren't enough for deterministic algorithms to break the $0.5$ bound and in fact using the degree information makes the performance worse\\
We can however, speaking broadly, conclude that the Bipartite Matching adversary is resistant to changes in the capacity of algorithms and breaking randomized and deterministic competitiveness bounds is easier by restricting the adversary with random arrivals or i.i.d graphs then by increasing algorithm capabilities\\
It is however notable, that in the case of Matching with Recourse, where the goal of the adversary is to 'fool' the algorithm repeatedly, an algorithm with recourse of $O(\log^2 n)$ is known, being close to the known lower bound of $O(\log n)$, despite the case for general vertex arrivals being hard
